# spermatocyte 6

*Author type `spermatocyte 6` in Li et al. 2022 (Fly Cell Atlas; 10x testis 202, Smart-seq2 testis 3; author term: FBbt:00004936 spermatocyte). Numbered spermatocyte cluster 6.*

## Summary
Spermatocyte 6 is one of the numbered spermatocyte clusters of the adult testis analysis. The authors treat the testis germline as a differentiation continuum (germline stem cells, proliferating spermatogonia, spermatocytes, maturing spermatids, late elongation-stage spermatids), and the numbered spermatocyte clusters are consecutive transcriptional states along the primary spermatocyte stage. The paper does not describe this cluster individually; the numbering order is the authors' and its precise position in the continuum is not stated.

## Markers
The paper gives no per-subcluster marker list. For the spermatocyte stage as a whole it reports a continuous rise in the number of genes transcribed, with strongly upregulated genes (kmg, Rbp4, fzo, can, sa and, in later spermatocytes, the Y-linked fertility factors kl-3 and kl-5) not substantially expressed in other cell types; late spermatocytes also show low-level transcripts of marker genes of other cell types (Upd1, eya, grh, Mhc, Hml). These are stage-level genes, not specific to this subcluster.

## Location
Testis (with seminal vesicle) of adult males; nuclei from 0-1 day old males. Primary spermatocytes sit in the 16-cell spermatocyte cysts surrounded by somatic cyst cells. The testis nuclei were FACS-sorted and the smallest (haploid spermatid) population was excluded from two of the three 10x runs.

## Function
Growth phase of male germ cells before meiosis; large-scale transcription of genes needed for meiosis and spermatid differentiation.

## Label provenance
Authors' ontology id FBbt:00004936 (spermatocyte); broad class male germline cell. Smart-seq2 cells (3) were typed by a classifier trained on the 10x annotation.

## Mapping notes
The call is to the general spermatocyte type; the numbering is an author-defined sub-state split of that one type (several clusters share the same term), not separate FBbt types. Data are adult (0-1 day testis).
