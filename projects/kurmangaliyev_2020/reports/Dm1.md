# Dm1

*Cluster `Dm1` (type `Dm1`, class N=neuron) in Kurmangaliyev et al. 2020
(GSE156455). Dm1 is an established optic-lobe cell type defined by classical
morphology and confirmed by scRNAseq.*

## Summary

Dm1 is a distal medullary wide-field amacrine interneuron of the adult optic
lobe. Its soma lies in the anterior region of the cell body rind of the medulla
cortex. The primary neurite branches extensively at the distal surface of the
medulla, arborizing at the boundary between layers M1 and M2 (in the same
sublayer as Dm18, more proximal than Dm9 and Dm10 in M1) and forming a
moderately broad arbor of approximately 20-30 columns with characteristic
bouton-like terminals from which short fine branches project. Individual arbors
overlap. There are approximately 40 Dm1 neurons per hemisphere. Dm1 is
glutamatergic (Davis et al., 2020) and is classified as a secondary Notch OFF
hemilineage neuron born from a medulla forming neuroblast during the
Hbn/Opa/Slp temporal window (Konstantinides et al., 2022).

## Markers

- Transcriptomic identity consistent with glutamatergic medulla interneuron.
- Cluster is named directly after the canonical morphological type Dm1
  (Fischbach and Dittrich, 1989; Morante and Desplan, 2008).

## Location

- **Neuropil:** medulla (distal layers, M1/M2 boundary sublayer).
- **Cell body:** anterior region of the medulla cortex cell body rind.
- **Cell class:** optic lobe amacrine interneuron, wide-field, multicolumnar
  (~20-30 columns, overlapping arbors).

## Function

- Medulla interneuron; wide-field lateral integration across the M1/M2
  boundary of the distal medulla. Glutamatergic neurotransmission. Bouton-like
  terminals suggest output specialisation within this layer.

## Mapping notes

- FBbt has a specific term "distal medullary amacrine neuron Dm1"
  (FBbt:00003768) whose definition matches this cluster precisely: same soma
  location (anterior cell body rind), same layer profile (M1/M2 boundary,
  same sublayer as Dm18), same moderately broad arbor size (~20-30 columns),
  same bouton-like terminal morphology, and same glutamatergic identity. No
  subtype ambiguity: Dm1 is not split into further subtypes in FBbt. This is
  an exact match.
