# Tm3

## Summary

Tm3 is a narrow-field transmedullary columnar neuron of the adult Drosophila optic lobe. It is a well-characterised cell type defined by its stereotyped arborisation pattern spanning the medulla and lobula neuropils and by its dual cholinergic and GABAergic neurotransmitter identity. The cluster represents the canonical adult Tm3 (Tm3a) neuron as described by Fischbach and Dittrich (1989) and subsequently refined by electron microscopy and transgenic labelling.

## Markers

- Cholinergic: VAChT (VGlut-ChAT) expression confirmed by anti-HA antibody detection of HA-tagged VAChT (Pankova and Borst, 2017)
- GABAergic: VGAT-GAL4 driver and anti-GABA antibody (Raghu et al., 2013)
- Dual neurotransmitter: capable of both cholinergic and GABAergic neurotransmission

## Location

- Optic lobe: medulla and lobula neuropils
- Dendrites (post-synaptic): fine arborisations in medulla layers M1, M4, and M5; bleb-type arborisations in medulla layers M1, M4, M9, and M10
- Axon terminals (pre-synaptic): lobula layers 1 and 4 with bleb-like terminal arborisations; additional pre-synaptic terminals in medulla layers M9-M10 and laterally extending neurites

## Function

Tm3 is an interneuron in the ON motion detection pathway of the visual system. It receives synaptic input from lamina monopolar neurons L1 and L5 and medulla intrinsic neuron Mi1, placing it downstream of the ON pathway photoreceptor channel. It provides output to T4 neurons (Takemura et al., 2013), which are the direction-selective motion detectors. Tm3 thus acts as an intermediate relay conveying luminance-increment (ON) signals from the lamina to the motion detection circuitry of the medulla and lobula.
