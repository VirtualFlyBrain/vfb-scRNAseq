# Cluster Report: Tm9a

## Dataset
Kurmangaliyev et al. 2020 (GSE156455) — transcriptional programs of visual system circuit assembly in Drosophila melanogaster.

## Summary
Tm9a is one of two transcriptomic subtypes (Tm9a, Tm9b) of Tm9 neurons identified in the Kurmangaliyev 2020 optic-lobe single-cell RNA-sequencing atlas. Tm9 neurons are narrow-field transmedullary interneurons of the adult visual system: they arborise dendritically in medulla layers M2-M3 (with extensions into M1, M4, M5) and project axons across the second optic chiasma to terminate in lobula layer 1, where they provide cholinergic input to T5 neurons. The Tm9a subtype is distinguished from Tm9b by expression of the Wnt ligand Wnt10, and targets the dorsal half of the lobula. Tm9b, by contrast, expresses Wnt4 and targets the ventral half. Both subtypes are present across all pupal timepoints sampled (24 h to 96 h APF), consistent with a developmental trajectory. Tm9a comprises approximately 1765 cells in the atlas.

## Markers
- Wnt10 (positive; discriminating marker for Tm9a vs Tm9b)
- Wnt4 (negative; marks the sibling Tm9b subtype)
- General Tm9 markers: sim (transcription factor), cholinergic markers (cha pathway)

## Location
Optic lobe — medulla (dendritic arborisations in layers M2 and M3 primarily) and lobula layer 1 (axon terminal, dorsal half).

## Function
Columnar cholinergic interneuron mediating visual motion processing. Receives input in the medulla and outputs in lobula layer 1 to T5 neurons (OFF-motion pathway). The dorsal/ventral regionalization of Tm9a vs Tm9b may reflect spatial mapping of visual field topography across the lobula.

## Cell count
1765 cells (atlas N, class N = neuron, type Tm9, subtype Tm9a).

## Notes
The Tm9a/Tm9b subtype split was newly described in this dataset. FBbt:00058233 ("transmedullary neuron Tm9a") was defined directly from Kurmangaliyev et al. 2020, with Wnt10 expression and dorsal lobula targeting as its defining criteria — identical to those of this cluster. This is an exact match by provenance: the FBbt subtype and this cluster share the same paper, the same discriminating marker (Wnt10), and the same anatomical criterion (dorsal half of lobula).
