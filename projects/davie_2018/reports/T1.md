# T1

*Cluster `T1` (Seurat res-2.0 cluster 37; 428 cells; ages 0-30 days) in Davie
et al. 2018 (GSE107451, 57K adult brain). Classical lamina/medulla feedback
neuron of the optic lobe.*

## Summary

T1 is a well-established optic-lobe columnar neuron. In this atlas it forms a
clean, single-identity cluster recovered by both marker-gene logic and
regression mapping against published bulk transcriptomes, with no ambiguity
flagged for the cluster.

## Markers

The paper singles out T1 as "the only neuronal type to express Eaat1"
(glutamate transporter), the basis for its identification (Hamanaka and
Meinertzhagen, 2010). Top Table S3 markers for res-2.0 cluster 37 include
Eaat1, CG2016, CG15522, CG14274, Octbeta2R, hbn, Crz, Mmp2, and Drep3. A small
separate GEO neuropeptide subcluster labelled "Crz" (9 cells) also maps back
to this same res-2.0 cluster 37, suggesting a minor corazonin-expressing
substate within/adjacent to the T1 population rather than a distinct type.

## Location

T1 is a columnar neuron of the lamina-medulla circuit, with processes in the
lamina and terminals in medulla layers M1-M2, classically described as
providing feedback from lamina to medulla in the ON motion pathway.

## Function

T1 is part of the ON-pathway visual motion circuitry, receiving input
associated with L1/photoreceptor signalling and feeding back onto the lamina
cartridge/proximal medulla; established from EM and dye-fill anatomy
(Fischbach and Dittrich, 1989; Hamanaka and Meinertzhagen, 2010).

## Label provenance

Table S2 annotates res-2.0 cluster 37 as "T1" (method: literature
markers/mapping; refs Hamanaka and Meinertzhagen, 2010; Konstantinides et al.,
2018). GEO label matches exactly (label_validation.tsv: "match"). No REVIEW
flag.

## Mapping notes

Single, well-defined morphological type; expect a single exact-match FBbt
term for "T1 neuron" at adult/optic-lobe granularity. The co-mapped Crz
subcluster is evidence of possible neuropeptide heterogeneity but is not
itself annotated as a separate cell type here, so it should not force a
finer split.
